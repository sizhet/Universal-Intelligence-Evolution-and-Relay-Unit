## 1. Start in 60 Seconds

**UIERU = Execution Path + Forward Guidance + Relay + Evolution.**

A **Universal Intelligence Evolution and Relay Unit (UIERU)** is a composable *organizational* unit for coordinating existing intelligence—human teams, LLMs, agents, tools, workflows, and services. It connects a governed **Calling Path** to a **forward-guiding Scoring Forest**, records execution and counter-evidence, packages what can be relayed, and proposes improvements for independent validation.

```text
Task / Problem
      |
      v
Contract (K): goal, scope, subject, authority, constraints
      |
      v
Calling Path (P) <---- forward guidance ----> Scoring Forest (F)
      |                choose / monitor / replan
      +---- optional Exploit / Explore / Challenge trails
      |
      v
Results + evidence + failures + unresolved residuals
      |
      v
Relay (R): handoff and portable structural portfolio
      |
      v
Evolution (E): candidate delta -> sandbox validation -> approval
      |
      +----> next task, next UIERU, or collective learning network
```

**Important:** UIERU is *not* a replacement for existing models, agents, or workflow engines; it is *not* necessarily autonomous; and it does *not* confer new authority on an executor. Adopt it where guidance, traceability, reuse, relay, or structural improvement outweigh coordination costs.

## 2. Understand It Through a Small Real-World Task

Suppose a homeowner asks a neighborhood crew to mow the lawn before Saturday.

| UIERU element | In the lawn-service task | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **K — Contract** | Job scope, schedule, safety constraints, access permission, acceptance criteria | Defines what is authorized and successful |
| **P — Calling Path** | Confirm -> check weather -> prepare -> travel -> mow -> inspect | Makes dependencies and handoffs explicit |
| **F — Scoring Forest** | Compare schedules, weather exposure, safety, cost, and customer impact | Guides choices *before and during* execution |
| **R — Relay** | Pass job status, access evidence, unresolved issues, and next steps | Prevents the next crew from starting blind |
| **E — Evolution** | Test a pre-visit gate-access checklist after repeated failures | Turns evidence into governed process improvement |

If the gate is locked, the team might consider obtaining authorized access, rescheduling, or assigning a different crew. A **Multi-Trail** design keeps these alternatives distinguishable, checks assumptions, and preserves counter-evidence. For a trivial task, however, a simple checklist may be better than running several sandboxes.

## 4. The Five-Part Canonical Unit

The proposed research model is:

\[
U = (K, P, F, R, E)
\]

- **K — Contract:** the goal, relevant subject and task object, permitted actions, constraints, accountability, and success conditions.
- **P — Calling Path:** a structured sequence or graph of executable actions, dependencies, tools, and agents.
- **F — Scoring Forest:** perspective-aware evaluation and *forward* guidance for choosing paths, allocating resources, monitoring risks, and revising decisions.
- **R — Relay:** a contract-governed exchange of status, evidence, provenance, alternative trails, residuals, and candidate structural knowledge.
- **E — Evolution:** the controlled lifecycle for discovering, testing, approving, deploying, and rolling back structural changes.

The five parts are conceptual roles, **not five mandatory services**. A lightweight implementation may combine several roles in one component. Conversely, a large deployment may distribute them across multiple independently governed systems.

## 5. The Key Engineering Distinctions

| Do not confuse | With |
|---|---|
| **Retrospective scoring** | **Forward guidance** that changes candidate selection and execution decisions |
| **Multiple speculative answers** | **Multi-Trail exploration** with explicit budgets, isolation, evidence, and termination |
| **Task handoff** | **Structural Relay** that preserves provenance, alternatives, failures, and residuals |
| **Successful output** | **Validated structural improvement** that generalizes to future tasks |
| **Transporting an intelligent structure** | **Transferring execution authority** — the latter requires separate authorization |
| **Shared world facts** | **Subject-indexed experiences** and private, context-dependent interpretations |
| **Scaling model capability** | **Scaling intelligence organization** across tools, agents, teams, and domains |
| **Recursive self-improvement proposal** | **Authorized deployment** of a tested improvement |

**Three non-negotiable governance principles:**

1. **Scoring cannot create authority.** A high-scoring action may still be prohibited.
2. **Portability is not permission.** Receiving a portfolio does not authorize execution, disclosure, or deployment.
3. **Self-evaluation is not sufficient approval.** Structural RSI requires suitable independent evidence, validation, and rollback controls.

## 6. An Implementation-Oriented Minimum

A useful first prototype can be deliberately small:

```yaml
uieru_id: example-lawn-service-001
contract:
  goal: Complete authorized lawn service before Saturday
  authority: Customer-approved access and service scope only
  constraints: [safety, weather, budget, access]
calling_path:
  - confirm_booking
  - check_weather_and_access
  - prepare_equipment
  - execute_service
  - inspect_and_handoff
scoring_forest:
  perspectives: [safety, customer_value, cost, reliability]
  decision_points: [schedule, access_exception, completion]
relay:
  include: [status, evidence, open_issues, alternatives, provenance]
evolution:
  candidate: pre_visit_access_confirmation
  deployment_policy: validate_then_approve
```

This YAML is **illustrative, not a normative conforming instance**. Consult [SPEC-001](specs/SPEC-001-Canonical-UIERU-Contract-and-Schema.md) for the proposed schema and semantic constraints.

A practical first test compares **(A)** a simple checklist, **(B)** a conventional workflow or agent orchestration, and **(C)** a lightweight UIERU with forward guidance and evidence-preserving relay. Measure task success, human effort, latency, cost, handoff fidelity, safety incidents, and the quality of validated improvements. Do not assume that option C wins.

## 9. What This Repository Does Not Claim

- It does **not** demonstrate that a universal organizational unit works better for every task or domain.
- It does **not** establish autonomous consciousness, subjective experience, or personal identity in AI.
- It does **not** prove that multi-trail search always improves quality relative to its cost.
- It does **not** equate structural changes with validated intelligence gains.
- It does **not** assume that every participant can or should share private memories, proprietary evidence, or evaluation principles.
- It does **not** present the proposed specifications as an adopted industry standard or a fully verified production implementation.

These are research proposals and engineering contracts intended for testing, refinement, and possible interoperability work.
